NISAR radar · Herat, Afghanistan · 19 June to 17 October 2026

Which fields kept growing through a summer with no rain?

Around Herat it does not rain from June to October, so land that grows a crop in those months is getting water from somewhere. NASA and ISRO's NISAR radar sees through cloud and dust and passed over the valley ten times. OFFBEAT reads those ten passes hectare by hectare and marks the patches whose season ran out of step with the fields beside them.

How far the evidence goes

OFFBEAT finds radar change and compares its timing between neighbours. It is not a drought map, an irrigation detector or a crop map. These are the limits to keep in mind while reading the map.

The answer moves with the rule

Each bar is the farmland that counts as changed under one version of the rule, fully recalculated. The standard rule is one reasonable choice, not the only one.

Nothing here has been checked against independent data

The numbers are what the radar measured under a stated rule. They are not yet confirmed on the ground or against other satellites, and no ground records exist for Herat.

Fields that fail together look normal

A hectare is only compared with its neighbours. If a whole area lost its water, every field would look like the next one and nothing would be marked.

Radar change does not prove irrigation

The echo rises when plants grow, and also when soil gets wetter or rougher. A summer peak fits a watered crop, but the radar cannot tell us the cause. That is why every interpretation on this page is labelled a hypothesis.

Grey does not mean no farming

Orchards and vineyards look much the same to the radar all summer, so they stay grey. So does land harvested before the first pass on 19 June.

One season, one missing pass

There is no earlier year to compare with, so a normal summer is unknown. The pass of 6 August is not in the archive and is not filled in. The data are marked provisional by NASA.

What would settle it

Sentinel-2 greenness: were plants visibly growing where the radar says a season happened? NASA rainfall (GPM): was it dry before each pass, so that rain can be ruled out? Both checks are planned and their results will be reported as they come out, including the misses.

Data

NISAR L-band GCOV, path 70 frame 19, provisional Level-2 products from NASA's Alaska Satellite Facility. NISAR is a joint NASA and ISRO mission. Radar power is averaged from 10 m to 100 m cells, and the scene-wide brightness shift on each date is removed.

Method

A hectare changed when its HV echo at least doubled between its weakest and strongest pass. A summer peak is strongest on 18 August or later; an early peak is strongest in June or July. A hectare is in step when its ten values rise and fall with the average of at least four changed hectares within about half a kilometre. The comparison follows the reasoning of Bazzi et al. (2019), who used it with Sentinel-1 in Catalonia.

About

OFFBEAT is a prototype for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026, challenge "Dancing with the SARs". Built with the assistance of Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic. No individual farms or people are identified; the smallest unit shown is one hectare.
