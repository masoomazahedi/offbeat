## The rules used

- A hectare **changed** when its HV echo grew at least 2.0 times (3.0 dB) between its weakest and strongest pass.
- **Summer peak** means the strongest pass was 2026-08-18 or later; **early peak** means June or July.
- **Out of step** means the correlation with the average of the changed hectares within 5 cells (about half a kilometre) is below 0.3.
- A hectare with fewer than 4 changed neighbours is not judged.
- A **place** is 2 or more connected out-of-step hectares.

## How far this has been checked

These numbers are what the radar measured under the stated rules. The project is not validated, but it is no longer unchecked.

**Checked.** Twenty hectares were drawn at random and compared against NASA optical imagery (HLS Sentinel-2, colour infrared) on 20 June, 19 August and 18 September 2026, with the calls recorded before the radar's answers were opened. Under the strict rule set in advance, **8 of 20 agree**. By class: **6 of 8** hectares the radar called a summer peak are visibly green in August or September; **2 of 7** it called quiet are bare, so four were green and the radar missed real growth; **0 of 5** early peaks passed a rule that required them to go bare by late summer, though all five were green in June as predicted. The check was AI-assisted rather than done by an independent person. Full method, per-point numbers and caveats: validation/RESULTS.md.

**Not checked.** The out-of-step rule, which is this project's main output, cannot be confirmed from optical imagery; it needs ground truth. Rainfall records (NASA GPM) have not been used, so rain before a pass has not been ruled out as a cause of a radar change.

Twenty points is a spot check, not an accuracy figure for the valley. See LIMITS.md.
